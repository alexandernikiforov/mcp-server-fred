Use the FRED MCP server to write a human-readable credit and market regime report in the exact order below.
Use only the available FRED MCP series and clearly state when a desired confirmation layer is not available from this server.

The report must be written for an individual investor, not for a trading system.
The report must be written in {language}.
The goal is to explain what is happening in credit, rates, funding, volatility, and liquidity without requiring the reader to inspect individual FRED series.

1. Data freshness

Start by stating the latest available observation date for the key series.
Mention clearly if any important series are stale, missing, or not directly comparable because of different publication dates.
Do not overinterpret stale data.

2. Credit system health

Review investment-grade credit first.

Use:
- BAMLC0A0CM for broad IG OAS
- BAMLC0A4CBBB for BBB OAS

Explain whether high-quality corporate credit is stable, widening, or under stress.
Explicitly answer the question: “Are lenders still willing to fund good borrowers?”
Keep this section focused on system health, not risk appetite.

3. Credit risk appetite

Review high-yield credit next.

Use:
- BAMLH0A0HYM2 for broad HY OAS
- BAMLH0A1HYBB for BB OAS
- BAMLH0A2HYB for B OAS
- BAMLH0A3HYC for CCC-and-below OAS

Explain whether investors are still willing to fund risky borrowers.

Distinguish between:
- normal discrimination against weak borrowers,
- broad high-yield deterioration,
- stress propagating upward from CCC to B, BB, BBB, and IG.

Explicitly state whether credit stress is isolated in weak credit or spreading through the capital structure.

4. Rates and curve

Review the Treasury and inflation-rate backdrop.

Use:
- DGS2
- DGS10
- DGS30
- T10Y2Y
- T10Y3M
- DFII10
- T10YIE

Explain what is driving pressure in rates, if any:
- inflation expectations,
- real yields,
- term premium,
- recession expectations,
- or a mixed regime.

Distinguish between:
- an uncomfortable long-end / real-rate regime,
- a recessionary bull-flattening regime,
- an inflationary bear-steepening regime,
- a funding-stress regime.

Explain what the rates backdrop means for long-duration assets and equity valuations.

5. Funding and liquidity

Review short-term funding and financial conditions.

Use:
- SOFR
- EFFR
- SOFR minus EFFR
- DCPF3M minus EFFR
- DCPN3M minus EFFR
- NFCI

Explain whether funding markets are normal, tightening, or stressed.
Explicitly answer the question: “Is there evidence of plumbing stress?”
Mention whether commercial paper and repo/funding indicators confirm or contradict the credit-spread picture.

6. Volatility

Review equity volatility.

Use:
- VIXCLS
- recent persistence above 20
- recent persistence above 25

Explain whether volatility is:
- ordinary equity noise,
- a caution signal,
- or evidence of broader market stress.

Do not treat a one-day VIX spike as a regime change.
Volatility must be interpreted as confirmation, not as the primary signal.

7. Regime classification

Assign classification for each layer:

- Credit:
- Funding:
- Rates:
- Volatility:
- Liquidity:

Then assign one overall regime using the portfolio rulebook terms:

- GREEN
- YELLOW
- RED
- BLACK

The regime label must be accompanied by a clear explanation. Do not leave the label unexplained.
The classification should be conservative.
If evidence is mixed, prefer the lower-severity classification and explain what confirmation is missing.
A regime should not be upgraded from GREEN to YELLOW, RED, or BLACK unless deterioration is persistent and confirmed across more than one layer.

8. Final verdict

Write a concise final verdict in normal prose.

The verdict must distinguish clearly between:
- price discomfort,
- equity volatility,
- long-end rate pressure,
- credit stress,
- funding stress,
- systemic liquidity stress.

Explicitly state whether the current regime looks like:
- functioning late-cycle markets,
- credit deterioration,
- funding stress,
- recession risk,
- or systemic stress.

9. Portfolio interpretation

Translate the market regime into portfolio-relevant interpretation, not mechanical instructions.

Explain what the regime implies for:
- scheduled core deployment,
- excessive tactical activity,
- long-duration Treasury exposure,
- cash / SGOV optionality,
- equity overlays,
- metals as insurance.

Do not give trade commands.
Do not introduce new investment ideas.
Do not recommend tactical trades unless they are directly necessary to explain the regime.

10. Monitoring checklist

End with a short checklist of what would change the assessment.
List the few indicators that would matter most over the next 1–4 weeks.

Focus on:
- IG OAS trend,
- HY OAS trend,
- BBB / BB / B / CCC propagation,
- SOFR / EFFR and commercial paper stress,
- NFCI direction,
- VIX persistence,
- curve and real-yield behavior.

The checklist should help the reader know what to watch next without checking every series manually.
